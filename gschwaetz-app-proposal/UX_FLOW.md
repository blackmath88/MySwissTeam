# UX flow

## Scene 0 — Entry

Brand:

**Gschwätz**

Tagline:

> Meet the argument before the label.

Primary CTA:

**Let's talk**

Secondary copy:

> No quiz. No party matching. No need to decide yet.

## Scene 1 — Topic setup

Short neutral issue summary.

Example structure:

- What is being decided?
- What changes if accepted?
- What stays the same if rejected?
- Why is the issue contested?

CTA:

**Meet the perspectives**

Do not ask the user for their vote intention yet.

## Scene 2 — Meet the avatars

Show 3 unlabeled people.

Each card has only:

- first name / fictional name
- short personal tone line
- no party
- no left/right tag
- no yes/no label

Example:

**Lea**  
"I care most about whether this actually works in practice."

**Jonas**  
"I keep coming back to individual freedom."

**Noura**  
"For me the distributional consequences are hard to ignore."

CTA:

**Talk to Lea**

## Scene 3 — Conversation

Three suggested modes:

### Understand
- What matters most to you here?
- What problem are you trying to solve?
- What do you fear losing?

### Challenge
- What is the strongest argument against your view?
- What are you least certain about?
- What could change your mind?

### Compare
- Where do you agree with Jonas?
- What trade-off separates your view from Noura's?
- Which facts do you interpret differently?

Voice and text should both work.

## Scene 4 — Micro-reflection

After 3–5 exchanges:

> Anything surprise you?

Options:

- I agreed more than expected
- I understand the concern better
- I still disagree
- I am less sure than before
- Nothing changed

No positive/negative scoring.

## Scene 5 — Perspective map

Show concepts the user explored:

- autonomy
- cost
- fairness
- institutional trust
- implementation risk

This is not a political profile of the user. It is simply a map of the themes present in the conversation.

## Scene 6 — Reveal

Title:

**Now meet the sources behind the conversation.**

Reveal:

- source organisations
- official material
- party / association / institution positions
- claim-level citations
- what was synthesis vs direct source position

Do not use a "Gotcha! You agreed with X" tone.

## Scene 7 — Continue

Options:

- Talk to another perspective
- Compare two perspectives
- Inspect the sources
- Ask one final question

## Interaction grammar

The avatar should always support these speech acts:

- explain
- clarify
- qualify
- acknowledge trade-off
- present strongest counterargument
- state uncertainty
- identify values
- cite source
- distinguish fact from interpretation
- say "I don't know"

## Anti-patterns

Never:

- score ideological consistency
- predict the user's political affiliation
- recommend a vote
- shame contradictions
- gamify conversion
- impersonate real politicians
- fabricate personal histories
